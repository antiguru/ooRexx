Phase 4 exclusions and deviations
=================================

Two sections, and the distinction between them is the whole point of the file.

  An EXCLUSION is work assigned to a later phase. It will exist one day, and a
  row names the phase that owes it.

  A DEVIATION is a permanent difference chosen on purpose. Nobody is coming
  back for it.

A deviation is therefore strictly worse than an exclusion, and the gate asserts
the SET of both sections rather than a count. Adding a row to either is a plan
amendment, not a file edit -- otherwise the file is an artifact of the phase
being gated, and anything that turns out hard can be excluded by editing it.

Phase 7 and Phase 10 delete their own exclusion rows as they deliver them. The
deviations section shrinks only if a decision is reversed, which is a plan
amendment too.

The gate reads "66 of the 81 builtins in builtinTable[] (BuiltinFunctions.cpp
:3042) are in scope, three of them partially, and the excluded set is exactly
this file".

---------------------

whole name would overstate the gap; ignoring the split would understate it.

DEVIATIONS -- permanent, chosen
-------------------------------

  1. DO OVER on a stem does not reproduce the oracle's traversal order.

     The oracle walks a balanced tree; we use a hash map. Measured, inserting
     tails 1, 2, 3, 10, ZZ, B, the oracle yields "1 B 3 2 ZZ 10" -- not
     insertion order, not ascending, not descending, not byte order. It is
     CompoundVariableTable's tree shape, and reproducing it means reproducing
     the tree, which is the entire cost the hash map exists to avoid.

     Justified by measurement rather than by appeal to ANSI leaving the order
     implementation-defined: DO OVER on a stem appears zero times in ooTest,
     zero times in CoreClasses.orx and StreamClasses.orx, and a handful of
     times in samples/windows/ under oodialog/ and ole/, of which at least two
     print in iteration order. All are Windows-only samples that no gate runs.

     Consequence, and it is a rule rather than an observation: NO CORPUS
     PROGRAM MAY CONTAIN DO OVER ON A STEM. Such a program could never pass,
     because the oracle's order is deterministic and ours is a different
     deterministic order. DO OVER on a string or a number is fine -- each
     iterates once, yielding itself.

  2. The EVALUATION-depth limit raises 11.1 where the oracle raises nothing.

     Read this row carefully, because the same error number is parity on two
     axes and a deviation on a third, and only the third belongs here.

     PARITY, not a deviation, on the parse-side axes: nested parentheses and
     nested calls both raise 11.1 in the oracle -- bisected at roughly 39,900
     parens and in [34,500, 34,760] calls -- and MAX_EXPR_DEPTH raises the same
     11.1 at 50,000 on one shared budget. Same construct, same condition, same
     number. Nothing to record but the fact that it matches.

     DEVIATION on the flat-term axis: the oracle evaluates a 100,000-term
     expression and prints its answer, then dies at 150,000 with SIGSEGV, rc
     139, no condition and no message at all. There is no number to reproduce.
     4a raises 11.1 above its evaluation-depth limit instead, because 11.1,
     "Insufficient control stack space", is the closest condition the language
     has to what actually happened.

     So: on parens and calls we match the oracle; on flat terms we report where
     the oracle crashes. Both use 11.1 and only the second is a difference.

     Second-order consequence worth stating: a limit ABOVE the oracle's own
     cliff would be worse than one below it, since it widens the window where
     we succeed and the oracle segfaults. That is why the recommended limit is
     100,000 -- the largest depth the oracle is measured to survive -- and why
     the check must fire above 100,000 rather than at it. A 100,000-term
     expression reaches depth 100,000, and that is precisely the depth the
     oracle handles.


EXPRKIND OWNERSHIP -- the six expression forms 4a does not evaluate
---------------------------------------------------------------------

Criterion 5 requires every out-of-4a `ExprKind` variant to name an owning
phase, and that owner has to be one of the phases the split table names --
"4b", "4c", "Phase 5" or "Phase 7", spelled exactly as the table spells them,
because `coverage.rs` and `loud.rs` assert the set and compare the strings.

The design spec assigns only one of the six outright: `Message` is Phase 5's
(the "assigned elsewhere" paragraph names it, and `InstructionKind::Message`'s
own doc says its `term` is always an `ExprKind::Message`, so the instruction
and the expression travel together). The other five are never individually
assigned anywhere in the spec -- the nearest sentence ("argument attachment
inside Call, QualifiedCall, Message, List and VariableReference is exercised
by 4b and 4c") names two phases jointly for five variants, which is not an
owner a `match` arm can return.

This gap was found and closed at Task 16 gate time, not before. Five of the
six rows below are a judgement call rather than a spec citation, made by the
team lead ("main") on request when Task 16 flagged the ambiguity as the one
place a wrong call quietly breaks criterion 5's policing.

  Call              4b. Genuinely split, and the split is recorded rather than
                    smoothed into one silent string: a Rexx function call
                    resolves internal-routine first, then builtin, then
                    external, so 4b delivers the internal-routine half and 4c
                    delivers the builtin half. The owner named is the phase
                    after which the variant stops failing loudly for SOME
                    call target, which is 4b -- an internal-routine call
                    evaluates as soon as 4b lands. A reader who reaches the
                    end of 4b and finds `f(1)` still loud for a builtin name
                    is seeing exactly this, not a regression.

  VariableReference 4b. `>x`/`<x` exists to be passed at a call site (Error
                    20.930 otherwise), and `USE` -- the instruction that reads
                    an argument on the receiving end -- is 4b's.

  QualifiedCall     Phase 5. `ns:name(...)` needs a resolved package/namespace
                    object; namespaces come from `::REQUIRES`, and "every
                    directive... [is] Phase 5's" per the split table's
                    assigned-elsewhere paragraph.

  ClassResolver     Phase 5. Explicitly: "environment symbols beyond .nil,
                    .true and .false are Phase 5's", and this variant is how a
                    namespace-qualified class name resolves.

  List              Phase 5. The general form builds an array, which is Phase
                    5's object model -- the same reasoning Task 14b's gap
                    report used when it argued against bringing `List` into
                    4a for the `SAY` case alone.

  Message           Phase 5, per the spec directly (see above).

This section is asserted the same way EXCLUSIONS and DEVIATIONS are: the set
of six names and their owners is fixed, and `coverage.rs`/`loud.rs` fail if
their own owner table drifts from it. Changing a row here is a plan amendment,
the same rule the rest of this file follows, because this is exactly the kind
of decision that has previously gone missing by living only in a test file's
comment.


KNOWN GAPS -- neither excluded nor deviated, just not closed yet
---------------------------------------------------------------

Recorded here so they are not rediscovered as mysteries. Each is a real
divergence with no owner assigned, which is a third status and should not be
confused with either section above.

This section is pinned ASYMMETRICALLY, deliberately, and the asymmetry is the
point. ADDING a row needs no amendment: recording a divergence you have just
measured should never require permission, or the incentive runs the wrong way
and gaps go unrecorded. REMOVING one does, and only two things justify it --
assigning an owner, which moves the row up into EXCLUSIONS, or a measurement
showing the divergence is gone. Deleting a row because it is inconvenient is
the failure the set assertion exists to prevent, and it applies here as much as
above.

  Prefix operator chains ("- - - -1") recurse in message_subterm, which the
  shared expression-depth budget does not guard. They abort natively between
  1,150 and 1,200 levels on a default 2 MiB thread. The oracle's cliff for this
  construct has not been measured, so the size of the divergence is unknown.

  Debug, PartialEq and Clone on Expr are still recursive, with cliffs measured
  at 2,000/2,050 for Debug and 2,100/2,200 for the other two. No non-test path
  reaches them on a deep tree today: the only production formatter is a
  panic!("... {other:?}") that fires when the parser is already panicking, so
  the worst case is a panic message becoming a silent abort.

  A depth counter protects a sized caller only. On a default 2 MiB thread the
  native abort arrives at 331 parens or 341 calls, long before any counter at
  50,000 could fire. Every in-tree consumer runs on the sized interpreter
  thread; a library embedder on a normal stack does not, and the long-term
  answer is a documented minimum stack or a sized entry point in rexx-parse
  itself rather than a smarter counter.

  A raised condition's report prints one clause echo per ACTIVATION-LIKE LEVEL,
  innermost first, and 4b Task 2 closed the INTERPRET half of this row. The
  CALL half is still open and belongs to 4b Task 3. The measured rule, in full,
  because the first version of this row said only "one echo per nesting level,
  innermost first" and that is not enough to implement from:

  * The unit is a level, NOT a block. An error three nested DOs deep echoes
    ONCE; the same error inside an INTERPRET fragment echoes twice, and inside
    a fragment inside a fragment three times.

  * Every echo of a fragment carries the ENCLOSING INTERPRET clause's line
    number, never a line of the fragment. Measured with a two-line file whose
    line 2 is interpret "say 2 & 1":

         2 *-* say 2 & 1
         2 *-* interpret "say 2 & 1"
    Error 34 running /abs/frag.rex line 2:  Logical value not 0 or 1.
    Error 34.901:  Logical value must be exactly "0" or "1"; found "2".

    The MAJOR line names the innermost entry's line, which for a fragment is
    the same number and for a CALL is not (see below).

  * Each echo's indent is ABSOLUTE and is NOT 2 x the depth of the stack. A
    fragment's clauses sit at the enclosing INTERPRET clause's own printed
    indent -- delta 0 -- plus whatever nests them INSIDE the fragment, EXCEPT
    after any repetitive DO/LOOP that completed a pass and then ended on a
    failing control test, whose decrement crosses the fragment boundary
    outward and whose scope rule is in the separate row below (siblings
    accumulate, nesting does not):
    interpret "do jj = 1 to 1; say 2 & 1; end" at top level echoes the inner
    clause at 2 and the INTERPRET at 0, and the identical fragment two DOs
    deep echoes them at 6 and 4.

  * A CALLED ROUTINE's base is the calling clause's printed indent PLUS TWO,
    which is what makes this a different quantity from the fragment's and why
    it cannot be derived from a depth counter. Measured, all four combinations:

      caller printed indent | callee's own depth | callee clause echoes at
      ----------------------+--------------------+------------------------
      0  (call at top level)| 0 (flat routine)   | 2
      0                     | 1 (one DO)         | 4
      4  (call two DOs deep)| 0                  | 6
      4                     | 1                  | 8

    and two nested calls stack three echoes: say 1/0 at 4, call sub2 at 2,
    call sub1 at 0. The major line names the INNERMOST line (a raise on line 8
    of a routine called from line 3 reports "line 8", not "line 3").

  4b TASK 2 built the stack (Raised carries a Vec of sites; run.rs's
  seal_site_level closes a level), and with it the INTERPRET half is measured
  matching on: a raise inside fragment text, a raise inside a fragment inside
  a fragment, a LEAVE/ITERATE that escapes fragment text, and the same shapes
  under TRACE where nothing is raised at all. WHAT REMAINS is CALL: Task 3 owns
  pushing a level with the +2 base above, and the mechanism is already there
  (Interp::indent_offset for the base, Interp::clause_line_override for the
  line, seal_site_level for the first-wins boundary).

  HISTORY, kept because the reason this row gave for being unreachable was
  falsified once already. It was recorded as unreachable from any corpus
  program, on the grounds that the only construct nesting an instruction loop
  inside another was the INTERPRET fragment spike, which 4b deletes and which
  run_program does not expose. TASK 10 FALSIFIED THAT REASON (commit addf89b1):
  IF and SELECT resolve their whole construct inside their own step arm through
  a nested bounded loop, so nesting is ordinary rather than exotic.

  A fragment that fails to PARSE is the one piece of the INTERPRET half still
  open, and it is one line. 4b Task 2 made such a fragment raise the oracle's
  own condition (interpret "do forever then" is 27.901 at rc 229, where it used
  to be a loud rc 120), and the enclosing echoes match, but the oracle ALSO
  echoes the failing fragment clause -- at indent 0 whatever the enclosing
  indent, since a parse-time echo carries no indent at all:

         2 *-* do forever then
         2 *-*   interpret "do forever then"      (enclosing DO: indent 2)

  We emit the second line only. Reproducing the first needs the failing
  clause's TEXT, and rexx-parse's ParseError carries the clause's START byte
  with no end, so there is no span to cut; guessing one is right for a
  single-clause fragment and silently wrong for interpret "do jj = 1 to 1; do
  forever then; end", whose echo is "do forever then;" and not the rest of the
  text. Closing it means a clause span on ParseError. The same missing span is
  why a TOP-LEVEL syntax error still emits no echo either, and a second
  obstacle applies only there: parse_program takes the text by value and
  returns no ProgramSource on the failure path, so execute cannot even resolve
  the LINE for the major line without a rexx-parse signature change.

  Also still unsubstituted: a parse error's sub-message renders its catalogue
  template with &1 in it (found "&1" rather than found "THEN"), because
  ParseError deliberately carries no substitution values. rexx-parse's own
  error.rs module note has the measurement behind that decision and records
  that Phase 4 owes them for condition('o')~additional regardless.

  A Controlled (TO-style) loop's re-tested pass omits two >>> value lines that
  the oracle emits for its control variable. Measured, and read from the C++

  A SECOND SYMPTOM OF THE SAME RE-TESTED PASS, and it is not about tracing at
  all: the *-* indent is NOT a pure function of lexical nesting. A repetitive
  DO/LOOP that COMPLETES A BODY PASS and then ends on a FAILING CONTROL TEST
  decrements the oracle's indent counter one time too many, so later clauses
  print two spaces lower than their lexical depth -- see the qualification and
  scope rules below, both of which have been stated wrongly more than once.
  Found by review of 4b Task 2 and re-measured independently. No INTERPRET and
  no CALL involved:

    do
    do jj = 1 to 1
    nop
    end
    say 1/0          <- oracle prints at 0; we print at 2
    end

  Which preceding block shapes do it, `say 1/0` one `do` deep, every row
  measured against the oracle:

      preceding block                        | oracle | ours
      ---------------------------------------+--------+------
      do jj = 1 to 1 ... end (controlled)    |   0    |  2
      do 2 ... end (repeat count)            |   0    |  2
      n=0; do while n = 0; n = 1; end        |   0    |  2
      n=0; do until n = 1; n = 1; end        |   0    |  2
      do jj = 1 to 1 while jj < 5 ... end    |   0    |  2
      do label q jj = 1 to 1 ... end         |   0    |  2
      do forever ... leave ... end           |   2    |  2
      n=0; do while n = 0; n = 1; leave; end |   2    |  2
      do while 0 = 1 ... end (zero-trip)     |   2    |  2
      do jj = 1 to 0 ... end (zero-trip)     |   2    |  2
      do 0 ... end (zero-trip)               |   2    |  2
      do jj = 1 to 3; leave; end             |   2    |  2
      if 1 = 1 then nop                      |   2    |  2
      select ... end                         |   2    |  2
      plain do ... end                       |   2    |  2

  THE RULE: any repetitive DO/LOOP that COMPLETES AT LEAST ONE BODY PASS and
  then ends because a CONTROL TEST FAILS decrements once. Count exhausted,
  WHILE false and UNTIL true all qualify. A zero-trip loop, a loop left by
  LEAVE, and a non-repetitive block do not.

  THE DISTINGUISHING PROPERTY IS "A PASS COMPLETED", NOT "A RE-TEST FAILED."
  An earlier revision of this row said "exactly the two shapes that run out of
  iterations" and cited the zero-trip row as showing the decrement belongs to
  a failing re-test. Both are wrong, and in the same way: a zero-trip loop's
  FIRST test also fails, so that row cannot separate the two hypotheses. The
  WHILE and UNTIL rows above are what separate them, and they were measured
  only after the narrow rule had been written into four places in the tree.

  SCOPE: SIBLINGS ACCUMULATE, NESTING DOES NOT. Measured, all at static

  THE MECHANISM, READ FROM THE C++ RATHER THAN INFERRED FROM THE TABLE. Four
  attempts to state this as a rule about constructs all drifted; the cause is
  a mutable counter with two inconsistent exit paths, and naming the cause is
  the only statement that has not had to be corrected.

    settings.traceIndent is a counter on the activation
      (execution/ActivationSettings.hpp:188).
    newBlockInstruction increments it (execution/RexxActivation.hpp:308) and
      DoBlock's constructor saves the pre-increment value
      (instructions/DoBlock.cpp:71).
    A loop that ends NORMALLY calls terminate ->
      terminateBlockInstruction(doblock->getIndent())
      (instructions/BaseDoInstruction.cpp:161), which is an ABSOLUTE RESTORE
      of the saved value (execution/RexxActivation.hpp:306).
    A loop whose CONTROL TEST FAILS calls endLoop
      (instructions/BaseDoInstruction.cpp:377), which does popBlockInstruction
      then a BARE unindent() -- no restore. unindent() clamps at 0
      (execution/RexxActivation.hpp:318), and that clamp is the floor.

  So the stray decrement is a C++ DEFECT: two exit paths for one operation,
  one restoring and one backing off relatively. Every row of both tables above
  falls out of which path ran.

    +++ "LINUX COMMAND <absolute path>"        (before the first clause echo)

  not an exclusion: the design spec's own Risks section required measuring
  what the oracle does with `?` and no tty and then either reproducing it or
  failing loudly, and neither happened.

  THE REASON IT WENT MISSING IS THE POINT, and it is the sixth instance of one
  mechanism in this phase: the requirement lived only in the spec's Risks
  section and never reached any task body. Briefs are extracted per task, so a
  decision recorded outside a task's own section is invisible to the person
  who would act on it. Recording it here rather than in a report because this
  file is where an owner looks.

  Owner unassigned. Reproducing the banner needs a decision about whether 4a's
  TRACE claims interactivity it cannot deliver; failing loudly on `?` is the
  cheaper alternative the spec already sanctioned.


CLOSED DEFECTS -- measured wrong, measured again, now matching
-------------------------------------------------------------

A fourth status, and the reason it is a section rather than a deletion: a
divergence that was found, fixed and verified leaves nothing behind for a
reader to trip over EXCEPT the temptation to "simplify" the fix. Each row says
what the oracle does, what we used to do, and which test would go red if the
fix were removed. Removing a row here needs the same amendment a KNOWN GAP does.

  THE *-* CLAUSE ECHO SATURATES AT 40 COLUMNS OF INDENT (fixed, 4b Task 2).
  Nesting depth drives the echo's indent at two spaces per level and that
  growth stops at 40. Measured with plain nested DOs around one failing clause
  and no call or INTERPRET anywhere: depth 18 gives 36, 19 gives 38, 20 gives
  40, and 21, 25 and 30 all give 40. Before the fix this crate was uncapped and
  printed 42, 50 and 60 for those last three -- a 4a divergence, not a 4b
  regression, and one no program in the 4a subset nested deeply enough to see.

  THE CAP IS ON THE *-* ECHO ALONE, AND ON THE TOTAL. Two things follow, and
  both were measured rather than assumed:

  * Value lines are NOT capped. Under trace r at depth 25 the *-* echo prints
    40 while the >>> line for the same clause prints its full 50. So a clamp
    inside static_indent, or applied to the shared push_indent, is wrong in
    both directions -- it would silently truncate every >>> as well.

  * The cap applies AFTER an activation base is added, not to either part. 20
    nested DOs around interpret "do jj = 1 to 1; say 1/0; end" would put the
    fragment's own clause at 42 and the oracle prints 40.

  The fix is one clamp in trace::push_clause (MAX_CLAUSE_INDENT), which is now
  the only *-* formatter in the crate: error.rs's Raised::report used to hold a
  byte-identical second copy and calls this one instead. Witnesses:
  rust/corpus/lang/deep_nesting_indent_cap.rex compares the whole report
  against the live oracle at depth 25, and error.rs's
  the_clause_echo_saturates_at_forty_columns pins 36/38/40/42/50 directly.
  Setting MAX_CLAUSE_INDENT back to usize::MAX takes the corpus from 31 of 31
  to 30 of 31, naming that program.
