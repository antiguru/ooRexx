### The hazard, and what it actually rests on

The brief asked for the reachable set of target shapes to be asserted, so that
implementing a message target later reddens a test instead of taking a wrong
path silently. **This specialisation does not rest on that set**, and saying
so is more useful than asserting a set it does not use: the fast path is keyed
*positively* on `ExprKind::Variable` with a bound slot. `Stem`, `Compound`
and the unimplemented `Message` all still reach `assign_expr_target` and
answer exactly as before, including its `Loud::expression` refusal and its two
`debug_assert!(at.is_none(), ...)` tripwires, which now never see a slot from
this caller because `PARSE` passes `None`.

What a slot write *does* rest on is that an `ExprKind::Variable` names a
simple variable, since the slot it writes is the whole name's. That is
asserted at the site:

    debug_assert!(
        matches!(shape_of(code.symbols.name(id).as_bytes()), NameShape::Simple),
        "a bound slot was written for {}, which is not a simple variable", ...
    );

It fires only in the debug gate, which is G6.

A deliberate `debug_assert` naming `{Variable, Stem, Compound}` on the general
path was considered and **rejected**: a message target is a loud refusal today
(`exit 120`), so such an assertion would turn a correct refusal into a debug
panic for a program this change does not affect.

## Candidate 4 -- measured -0.2340% against an attributed 0.326%

`bb81ae5221d6a48c64af2b27ec71c864e281267f`.

`let traced = self.traced_mode();` above the per-target loop, and the three
sites that had their own gate -- the `>=>` line inside the write, the `>>>`
line after it, and a `.` placeholder's `>.>` -- read the hoisted copy.
`assign_bound_variable`'s `rendered` became an `Option<&[u8]>` for the reason
`assign_expr_target`'s already is: `None` means no line will print it, and it
is now the caller that knows.

**Scope: once per trigger, not once per `PARSE`.** That is where
`ParseTrigger::parse` decides it (`ParseTrigger.cpp:248` and `:290`, two
copies of the assignment loop), and it is the scope whose safety needs no
claim about anything: nothing `assign_targets` reaches runs Rexx code.
A whole-`PARSE` hoist would have to cross `apply_trigger`'s `eval` of the
trigger operand and a comma fence's `required_string_value`, both of which can
enter Rexx. The property is asserted rather than argued -- a
`debug_assert_eq!(traced, self.traced_mode(), ...)` inside the loop, which
reddens the debug gate if a later change reaches Rexx code from in there.

**The shortfall against the ceiling is the scope, and re-derived at this scope
the measurement slightly beats the ceiling rather than missing it.** The
scout's 86,800,050 of gates includes `:600` and `:679`, which are outside the
per-target loop and were not touched, leaving 77,280,048 in the loop; a
residue of one gate per trigger at the 8 Ir the scout priced is 31,360,144, so
the in-loop, per-trigger ceiling is **45,919,904** and the measurement is
47,957,027, 104% of it. Against the brief's per-`PARSE` 68,880,034 it is 70%.

Visible in the profile: `run.rs:2704`, the `>=>` gate inside the write, falls
22,400,012 -> 11,200,006.

The remaining per-`PARSE` gain, about 13M or 0.06%, is below the brief's
threshold on its own and would cost the assertion above. It is not worth
taking.

## Candidate 5 -- measured +0.0324%, and it is slower

**Not committed.** Attributed 23,986,680 (0.113%) of saving; measured
+6,624,562 (+0.0324%) of cost.

The change is the one the scout described: `&cursor.string()[piece]` taken
once, inside the `Some(target)` arm, and the three readers given the slice
instead of the range. It was inside that arm and not above the `match` on
purpose -- the `.` placeholder arm reads the bytes only under `intermediates`,
and hoisting above the match would have added an index to every placeholder.

Three interleaved rounds, because the sign matters and the first delta was
only ~3x the c4 spread:

    c4  20,444,976,775   20,447,301,749   20,446,440,737
    c5  20,452,528,934   20,452,947,921   20,453,116,091

**Every c5 run is above every c4 run.** The mean delta is +6,624,562.

Where it went, from the same dumps:

* `exec_parse`'s own text is flat: 434,560,308 -> 434,840,309.
* Its inclusive cost rises 2,803,777,215 -> 2,811,331,504, +7,554,289.
* `slice/index.rs` inlined into `exec_parse` rises **+19,320,012**, and
  `num/uint_macros.rs` -- the cursor arithmetic -- falls -10,360,006.

So the hoist made slice indexing more expensive rather than less, which is the
opposite of the candidate's premise. **The mechanism is not established here**
and nothing was run to establish it; what is established is the sign and its
size.

**Candidate 4 had already taken most of what candidate 5 was sized against,
and the order decided which one was credited.** Before candidate 4 the piece
was indexed three times per assigned target. After it, on an untraced run,
`rendered` is `None` and the `>>>` branch is not taken, so two of the three
were already gone and only `self.text(...)`'s own index remained. Had the
brief's order been 5 then 4, the same total would have been credited the other
way round. The 0.113% ceiling was also a third of *all* slice indexing inlined
into `exec_parse`, and much of that total is `next_word`'s byte-at-a-time scan
of the source rather than the piece.
