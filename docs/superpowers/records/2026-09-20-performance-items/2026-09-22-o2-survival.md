# Does an optimisation found at `-O2` survive into `-O3` with fat LTO?

**No. Stop exploring at `-O2`.** One of the three landed optimisations
re-measured here **reverses sign at `-O2` on every axis it moves**, and a second
shows a 0.39% regression at `-O2` on an axis where it does nothing at all at
`-O3`. The brief asked me to stop and report if the sign does not survive, so
questions 2 and 3 are answered from the runs already made rather than pursued.

## Question 1 -- does the sign survive? No.

`26ccef4ee` (the driver's ops read through a slice cut to the loop's bound) is
a landed, kept optimisation. At `-O2` it is a regression on all three axes:

| axis | `-O3` fat LTO | `-O2` no LTO | |
|---|---|---|---|
| `rexxcps` | **-0.0741%** | **+1.0361%** | FLIP |
| `varlookup` | **-0.8644%** | **+3.0338%** | FLIP |
| `emptyloop` | **-0.7500%** | **+0.4863%** | FLIP |

The narrow axes reproduce to 0.0001%, so a 3.03% reading is about 30,000 times
the noise band. This is not a marginal call.

`725aa8863` (op fusion) keeps its sign on its own axis but **invents one on
another**: on `varlookup` it is inert at `-O3` (-0.0000%, 4,771 Ir out of
17.6e9) and reads **+0.3930%** at `-O2`.

Signs that did survive: op fusion on `rexxcps` (-1.6743% -> -1.9442%) and parse
in place on `rexxcps` (-0.6746% -> -0.5433%).

**Scoreboard: of the five (commit, axis) cells where the change does real work,
three flip sign.**

### The flip is the code, not a bad binary

Each commit was built **twice**, in separate `CARGO_TARGET_DIR`s. The flip was
re-measured end to end on the second, independent build:

| axis | profile | build `a` | build `b` |
|---|---|---|---|
| `rexxcps` | `-O2` | +1.0361% | +1.0321% |
| `varlookup` | `-O2` | +3.0338% | **+3.0338%** |
| `emptyloop` | `-O2` | +0.4863% | +0.4866% |
| `varlookup` | `-O3` | -0.8644% | -0.8645% |

Two separately built binaries, same sign, same magnitude to four decimals.

## Question 2 -- does the magnitude survive? Moot, and it would not have.

The `-O2`/`-O3` ratio per live cell: **1.161, 0.805, -13.990, -3.510, -0.648**.
Three are negative. There is no factor to correct by.

## Question 3 -- does `-O2` rank the same candidates? Not below the top five.

`varlookup` at `26ccef4ee`, top functions by self cost. The five hottest agree
in rank and roughly in share. Below them the two profiles disagree about what
exists:

| function | `-O3` self | `-O2` self |
|---|---|---|
| `RootSet::set_temp` | **0** | 456,000,444 (2.280%) |
| `RootSet::temp_at` | **0** | 456,000,426 (2.280%) |
| `RootSet::pop_frame` | **0** | 152,000,940 (0.760%) |
| **combined** | **0 (0.000%)** | **1,064,001,810 (5.319%)** |

Those three are inlined out of existence at `-O3`; at `-O2` they are the 6th,
7th and 8th hottest functions in the program. `-O2` reports 1,181 distinct
functions with nonzero self cost against `-O3`'s 977.

**5.319% of a phantom is larger than any delta any of these three commits is
worth.** An `-O2` profile would send someone to optimise calls the shipped
binary does not make.

## Method

Built **without editing `Cargo.toml`**, by environment override:

    CARGO_PROFILE_RELEASE_OPT_LEVEL=2 \
    CARGO_PROFILE_RELEASE_LTO=false \
    CARGO_PROFILE_RELEASE_CODEGEN_UNITS=16 \
    CARGO_TARGET_DIR=<own dir> cargo build --release -p rexx-exec --bin rexx-run

Confirmed at the rustc level from `cargo build -v`, on the `rexx-run` bin's own
line: `-O2` arm `opt-level=2 codegen-units=16 embed-bitcode=no`; `-O3` arm
`opt-level=3 codegen-units=1 lto=fat`. Both keep `debuginfo=2`. The worktree is
clean at `c418d4555`; nothing tracked was changed and nothing was committed.

Twenty builds: five commits x two profiles x two independent target
directories. **All ten `.text` hashes matched their rebuild; all twenty
whole-file hashes differed**, which is the `debug = true` path-in-debug-info
effect the brief predicted and the reason a whole-file hash cannot do this job.

    objcopy -O binary --only-section=.text <binary> <out> && sha256sum <out>

`0e9c9e062` serves as both the bounds-slice BASE and the parse-in-place HEAD:
`git diff 0e9c9e062 5e765dc5a` is **empty over `rust/`** and touches only two
documentation files overall, so one pair of binaries covers both roles. That
saved four builds and is asserted, not assumed.

Measurement, from a directory holding only the program being run (the
scratchpad is on the interpreter's external-routine search path):

    valgrind --tool=callgrind --callgrind-out-file=<out> <binary> <bench>.rex

**84 runs, every one `EXIT=0`**, figures from each dump's own `summary:` line.
60 runs are two rounds over ten binaries on three axes, arms of each pair
adjacent in time and round 2 in the reverse order of round 1; 24 more are the
independent-build control. Within-build spread over the two rounds, across all
42 cells: `emptyloop` 0.0000-0.0003%, `varlookup` 0.0000-0.0002%, `rexxcps`
0.0001-**0.0186%** (the widest cell is `0e9c9e062-o2-b`; the main set alone
tops out at 0.0059%). `rexxcps` is the noisy axis because it renders `TIME()`
into its own output, as the brief said.

**This matters for one cell and no others.** The bounds-slice `-O3` figure on
`rexxcps`, -0.0741%, is only about four times that worst-case spread. The flip
verdict does not rest on it: it rests on `varlookup` (3.0338% against a 0.0002%
spread) and `emptyloop` (0.4863% against 0.0003%), and on the `-O2` side of
`rexxcps` itself (+1.0361%, 56 times the spread).

The three `.rex` inputs are byte-identical at all six commits, checked by
`git rev-parse <commit>:<path>`, so every arm ran the same program.

The top-function figures come from a parser over the callgrind dumps whose
per-function output was **checked against `callgrind_annotate` and agrees to
the digit**, and whose summed self cost equals the dump's `summary:` line
exactly (100.0000%).

## What would make me distrust this

* **Three commits is a small sample, and two of the three are in the
  interpreter's dispatch path.** The flipping one, `26ccef4ee`, is a dispatch
  change. Parse-in-place is the one different in character and it kept its
  sign. I am not claiming a rate at which optimisations flip; I am claiming
  that one landed optimisation does, which is enough to answer the question
  that was asked.
* The two profiles differ in three ways at once (`opt-level`, `lto`,
  `codegen-units`). I did not separate which one causes the flip. The
  `RootSet` inlining evidence points at LTO plus `codegen-units`, but that is
  an inference from one axis at one commit, not a measured attribution.
* Everything here is retired instructions. No wall-clock claim is made.
